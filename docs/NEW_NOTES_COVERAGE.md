# Coverage of Lectures 7–13

This is an **incomplete** formalization. All included proof declarations are
checked by Lean, but that does not establish full coverage. “Partial” and
“restricted” below are not proofs of the unrestricted source statements.
No unproved theorem is exported as an assumption or disguised as a definition
of “regularity.” Source corrections are in [NEW_NOTES_AUDIT.md](NEW_NOTES_AUDIT.md).

## All nine numbered theorems

| Source | Status | What is actually established |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L7 T1, MLE consistency | **Corrected argmax theorem** | [MLEConsistency](../LectureNotes/MLEConsistency.lean): `argmax_consistency` proves convergence in probability from uniform criterion convergence, a well-separated population maximum, and maximization. `mle_maximizes_average_log` connects a positive likelihood MLE to the criterion. The source's three printed assumptions are insufficient; a uniform LLN and separation are not proved for every parametric density family. |
| L7 T2, asymptotic normality | **Scalar and vector IID theorems under explicit sufficient conditions** | [IIDScoreAsymptotics](../LectureNotes/IIDScoreAsymptotics.lean): `iid_mle_asymptotic_normality` derives the score CLT, curvature/envelope LLNs, and interior first-order equation from IID observations, derivatives, moment/information identities, and maximization. [MLEAsymptotics](../LectureNotes/MLEAsymptotics.lean) proves curvature control along the random estimation interval, using consistency and an integrable third-derivative envelope. Consistency, positive information, and measurable interior maximizers remain explicit hypotheses. The criterion is a supplied log-likelihood function; normalization and information identities must be checked for each model. [VectorMLE](../LectureNotes/VectorMLE.lean) now proves the vector theorem from IID observations, actual Fréchet derivatives, a Lipschitz derivative envelope, second moments, consistency, and interior maximization. The score covariance and negative expected curvature are kept distinct, yielding the sandwich covariance; the information identity specializes this to the inverse information matrix. Canonical Gaussian score limits, including singular covariance, are constructed in [VectorScoreCLT](../LectureNotes/VectorScoreCLT.lean). |
| L9 T1, Neyman–Pearson | **Proved for integrable dominated densities and randomized tests** | [NeymanPearson](../LectureNotes/NeymanPearson.lean): `StatisticalTest.neyman_pearson` and `neyman_pearson_necessity`. The necessity statement is almost everywhere, and the proof handles a zero threshold. `densityPower_eq_power` connects the weighted integral to the model measure, and `density_model_isProbabilityMeasure` proves normalization. [NeymanPearsonExistence](../LectureNotes/NeymanPearsonExistence.lean) proves existence and exact calibration for `0 < α < 1`, including points with zero null density and positive alternative density. |
| L9 T2, MLR UMP tests | **Proved for common strictly positive densities** | [MonotoneLikelihoodRatio](../LectureNotes/MonotoneLikelihoodRatio.lean): `mlr_lower_tail_threshold`, `mlr_lower_tail_power_antitone`, and `mlr_lower_tail_ump` prove composite-null optimality for a calibrated lower-tail rule. [MLRExistence](../LectureNotes/MLRExistence.lean) proves existence and exact calibration for interior sizes and nonincreasing power at arbitrary real cutoffs, even outside the statistic's range. Discrete, continuous, and mixed statistic laws are allowed. The common strictly positive density assumption remains explicit. The source's strict-monotonicity claim is corrected to nonincreasing power. |
| L10 T1, Wilks | **Scalar IID theorem, vector derivative theorem, and linear-subspace extension** | [WilksAnalytic](../LectureNotes/WilksAnalytic.lean): `iid_scalar_wilks` proves the χ²(1) limit of twice the maximized log-likelihood difference, using the same explicit IID, derivative, moment, consistency, and interior-maximization hypotheses. The estimator limit and quadratic expansion are derived, not assumed. The remainder bound follows from two mean value inequalities. [VectorWilks](../LectureNotes/VectorWilks.lean) derives the vector likelihood expansion from actual derivatives and proves point-null and moving-null limits. [ConstrainedVectorMLE](../LectureNotes/ConstrainedVectorMLE.lean) derives the joint full/restricted root limit from one score CLT, then proves a chi-square likelihood limit with codimension as degrees of freedom for a linear null subspace in identity-information coordinates. The restricted score is the actual projected full gradient. Consistency, stationary roots, curvature limits and Lipschitz envelopes are explicit. Curved restrictions and verification of general model regularity remain open. |
| L11 T1, Pratt | **Proved** | [ConfidenceSets](../LectureNotes/ConfidenceSets.lean): `pratt_identity` is Tonelli for a jointly measurable confidence graph, permitting infinite expected length. `pratt_ump_confidence_minimal` proves optimal expected volume assuming a UMP test for each singleton null, with a reference measure having null singletons. The claimed two-sided Student-test application is not asserted. |
| L12 T1, posterior normal approximation | **Scalar and vector theorems under explicit sufficient conditions** | [PosteriorAsymptotics](../LectureNotes/PosteriorAsymptotics.lean): `centered_posterior_gaussian_limit` proves a uniform bound over all measurable events for the actual centered/scaled posterior. Local quadratic log-likelihood convergence, prior continuity/positivity, and a global integrable envelope are explicit sufficient conditions. The change of variables and normalization are proved. `centered_posterior_gaussian_limit_ae` states almost-sure sampling convergence when these conditions hold almost surely. Gaussian-calibrated local events have asymptotically correct posterior content. [NormalPosteriorLimit](../LectureNotes/NormalPosteriorLimit.lean) verifies the analytic conditions for quadratic normal-mean likelihoods with bounded continuous integrable priors positive at the truth. [PosteriorInformationIntervals](../LectureNotes/PosteriorInformationIntervals.lean) transfers the uniform scalar approximation to the displayed interval with consistent positive estimated information, including an almost-sure pathwise version and a normal-mean-kernel application under explicit prior and center-consistency assumptions. [EstimatedInformationIntervals](../LectureNotes/EstimatedInformationIntervals.lean) separately proves pointwise frequentist coverage from a matching Gaussian estimator limit and consistent positive information. [VectorPosteriorAsymptotics](../LectureNotes/VectorPosteriorAsymptotics.lean) proves the Euclidean vector extension with actual affine Jacobians and covariance `A Aᵀ` for any invertible scale factor. [VectorNormalSamplePosterior](../LectureNotes/VectorNormalSamplePosterior.lean) derives the uniform-event approximation almost surely for the actual IID Gaussian location experiment with bounded continuous proper priors positive at the true mean; the sample-mean center consistency follows from the strong law. General IID posterior regularity beyond these explicit conditions remains open. |
| L13 T1, posterior-risk minimization | **Proved for measurable minimizers** | [BayesianDecision](../LectureNotes/BayesianDecision.lean): `bayes_risk_eq_expected_posterior_risk` uses the actual posterior Markov kernel and prior predictive distribution; `posterior_minimizer_is_bayes` proves optimality, allowing an exceptional predictive-null set and infinite nonnegative risk. It does not assume existence of a measurable selector for arbitrary action spaces. |
| L13 T2, James–Stein | **Proved for p ≥ 3 and positive variance** | [JamesStein](../LectureNotes/JamesStein.lean): `jamesStein_risk` proves the exact risk formula and `jamesStein_strict_improvement` proves strict improvement at every mean vector. Independent normal coordinates on an arbitrary sample space are covered by `jamesStein_risk_of_independent_normals`. Gaussian integration by parts, the smooth regularized risk, inverse-square integrability via Fatou, and the singular limit via dominated convergence are proved. [SteinDecision](../LectureNotes/SteinDecision.lean) proves finite loss integrals, dominance and inadmissibility for the actual extended-real risk. [NormalMinimax](../LectureNotes/NormalMinimax.lean) proves the minimax lower bound against all measurable rules using diffuse proper normal priors; the identity and James–Stein rules attain the value `p v`. [PositivePartStein](../LectureNotes/PositivePartStein.lean) proves positive-part dominance, James–Stein inadmissibility among measurable rules, and the same minimax value. The positive-part risk inequality holds at every mean, with strict improvement proved at zero. |
